**Goal:** Confirm everyone is signed in on a paid plan, get oriented to the three desktop tabs, and agree on one safety rule — all in 15 minutes. **Timebox: 0:00–0:15.**

This is the warm-up. The aim is not to learn everything; it is to make sure the app is open, working, and signed in, so the group can move straight into a real task. Resist the urge to explore. Momentum into [your first Cowork task](/ai/desktop/session-2-first-cowork-task/) matters more than a thorough tour.

## Confirm sign-in and plan

Before anything else, make sure the desktop app is open and you are signed in to a **paid plan**. Cowork and Code are paid-only features — the free plan does not include them. If you are not signed in, fix it now; this is the single most common thing that derails the first ten minutes.

1. Open the Claude desktop app. On Windows, launch it from the **Start menu**.
2. Check the top of the window for your account. If you see a sign-in prompt, sign in.
3. Confirm your plan supports Cowork and Code. Pro is the entry plan ($17/month billed annually, $20 month-to-month) and includes Claude Code; Team includes both Code and Cowork. If you are on the free plan, the Cowork and Code tabs will not be usable.

:::tip[For the facilitator]
**Say this:** "Before we do anything, let's make sure everyone is signed in. Look at the top of your window — do you see your name or a sign-in button?"
**Do this:** Scan the room. Sign-in problems are the #1 time sink — solve them now, not at minute 12.
**Watch for:** Free-plan accounts. If someone can't open Cowork or Code, that's the cause. Note it and pair them up rather than stalling the whole group.
**Timebox:** ~3 minutes. If one person is stuck, keep the group moving and circle back.
:::

## A quick tour of the three tabs

The desktop app has three tabs across the top. You do not need to master them today — just know what each one is for.

- **Chat** — general conversation. This is plain back-and-forth with Claude, like asking a question or drafting an email. Chat has **no access to your files**.
- **Cowork** — an autonomous assistant for knowledge work. It can read and write files in folders you connect, build spreadsheets and slide decks, and run its work in an isolated virtual machine separate from your computer. This is where most of today's work happens.
- **Code** — an interactive coding assistant with direct access to your local files. You review and approve each change as a visual diff. No terminal required. We'll take a brief look at this later.

Today the group will spend most of its time in **Cowork**, with a short look at **Code** near the end. Chat is the one you have likely already used.

:::tip[For the facilitator]
**Say this:** "Three tabs. Chat is what you know. Cowork is the new one — it can actually work with your files. Code is for later. That's it."
**Do this:** Click each tab once so everyone sees where they are. Don't demo features yet.
**Watch for:** People wanting to dive into Cowork immediately. Hold them — the safety rule comes first.
**Timebox:** ~4 minutes. Keep it to a pointing tour, not a feature walkthrough.
:::

## The one safety rule

Set this before touching Cowork, and it carries through every session:

**Claude proposes; you approve. And verify anything factual.**

Cowork shows you a **plan** and waits for your approval before acting. The recommended setting is **"Ask before acting"** — Claude asks permission before each step. There is a riskier **"Act without asking"** mode that works on its own; leave that off while you're learning. Claude also requires explicit permission before it permanently deletes any file.

The second half matters just as much for academic work: **Claude can fabricate.** It can invent citations, page numbers, quotes, and data values that look completely plausible. Nothing it produces is trustworthy until you have checked it against the original source. Treat every factual claim, reference, and number as a draft to verify — not a result to publish.

:::caution
A safety detail to know but not dwell on today: the folder and network permissions you set do **not** apply to web fetch, web search, or connected tools (MCPs, including Claude in Chrome). Those reach the internet regardless. Mention it; the full treatment lives in [Cowork, Safely](/ai/desktop/cowork-safely/).
:::

:::tip[For the facilitator]
**Say this:** "One rule for everything we do: Claude proposes, you approve — and you verify anything factual. It can make up citations and numbers that look real. Always check the source."
**Do this:** Confirm everyone's Cowork mode is set to "Ask before acting," not "Act without asking."
**Watch for:** Nods that mean "sure, sure" — this is the rule that protects research integrity. Make it land in one clear sentence.
**Timebox:** ~4 minutes.
:::

## Confirm the practice folder

Earlier prep asked everyone to create a small practice folder with a few sample files. Confirm it exists now — Cowork works inside folders you connect to it, so the first real task needs something to point at. If someone skipped this, see [Before We Meet](/ai/desktop/before-we-meet/) and have them create a quick folder with two or three files so they're not empty-handed.

:::tip[For the facilitator]
**Say this:** "Does everyone have their practice folder from the prep? Two or three files is plenty."
**Do this:** Get a quick thumbs-up around the room. For anyone without one, a 60-second folder with a couple of files is fine.
**Watch for:** Empty hands. The next segment needs a folder to connect; nobody should arrive without one.
**Timebox:** ~2 minutes. Then move straight into the first task — don't let orientation sprawl.
:::

## Watch for plan-gate prompts

As the group moves into real work, some actions may trigger a prompt to upgrade or confirm the plan. That's expected on paid features. Note it, keep moving, and sort out any genuine plan issue afterward rather than stopping the session.

Once everyone is signed in, oriented, and holding a practice folder, move on to [your first Cowork task](/ai/desktop/session-2-first-cowork-task/).
