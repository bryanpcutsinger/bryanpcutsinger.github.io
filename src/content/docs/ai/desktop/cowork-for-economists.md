Once you understand how Cowork works — it reads and writes files in the folders you connect, shows you a plan, and waits for your approval — the natural next question is *what should I actually use it for?* This page is a menu of realistic jobs for an economics professor who also chairs a department. Nothing here is exotic; it's the file-shuffling, drafting, and tidying that eats your week.

A few ground rules before the list. Cowork works inside [the folders you connect](/ai/desktop/meet-cowork/), so point it at a copy of the materials you want it to touch, not your only copy. It can build Excel files with working formulas and assemble PowerPoint decks. And as always, you stay in the loop: it proposes, you approve. For the research items especially, read the integrity reminders in [Use Cowork Safely](/ai/desktop/cowork-safely/) — they are not optional.

## Teaching

The teaching tasks that benefit most are the repetitive, format-heavy ones: turning a pile of one thing into a clean version of another thing.

- **Ask Cowork to** draft or revise a syllabus to match a department template — fold last term's syllabus into the current calendar, update office hours and dates, and flag anything the template requires that's missing.
- **Ask Cowork to** build a grading rubric as a spreadsheet — criteria down the rows, performance levels across the columns, point weights that sum correctly, ready to hand to a TA.
- **Ask Cowork to** generate variants of a problem set with a matching answer key — say, three versions of an intermediate-micro assignment with different numbers so the structure is identical but the answers differ.
- **Ask Cowork to** turn a folder of lecture notes into a slide deck, then summarize a stack of end-of-term student-feedback PDFs into a short list of recurring themes.

For anything quantitative — the problem-set answer key, a worked example — treat Cowork's math the way you'd treat a sharp but unchecked TA: spot-check the solutions yourself before they reach students. It can make arithmetic and reasoning errors with complete confidence.

## Research

Research is where Cowork can save real time *and* where the stakes for accuracy are highest. Use it for the mechanical handling of your materials, and verify every fact it produces.

- **Ask Cowork to** pull the tables and figures out of a dense PDF into a spreadsheet so you can check the numbers — a fast first pass at data entry, not a substitute for confirming each value against the original.
- **Ask Cowork to** organize and deduplicate a messy folder of working papers — group by topic, flag near-duplicates, and produce an index — without deleting anything until you've reviewed the list.
- **Ask Cowork to** draft a first-pass synthesis of a literature you point it at, as a starting outline you will verify.
- **Ask Cowork to** tidy a raw data folder into a clean, documented derived file — and to leave the raw files untouched.
- **Ask Cowork to** draft a referee-response letter or a revision memo from your own notes on what you changed.

:::danger[Verify everything; protect under-review work]
Claude can fabricate citations, page numbers, quotes, and data values, and present them as fact. Nothing it writes about the literature or your data is trustworthy until you have checked it against the original source. Two non-negotiables for this audience: (1) re-verify every number, citation, and quote before it enters a draft; and (2) do not put confidential, under-review, or referee material into any external service — keep that work to the materials and folders you control. The same integrity rules are laid out in [How Cowork Works Safely](/ai/desktop/cowork-safely/).
:::

A practical division of labor: let Cowork do the *moving and reformatting* of your research materials (extracting, organizing, drafting from your notes), and keep the *judgment* — what's correct, what's citable, what's defensible — entirely with you.

## Running the department

The chair's inbox is full of spreadsheets and documents that arrive messy and have to leave clean. This is squarely in Cowork's wheelhouse, and it's low-stakes enough to be a comfortable place to start.

- **Ask Cowork to** reconcile rosters and enrollment from several messy exports — match students across files, flag mismatches and duplicates, and produce one clean combined sheet.
- **Ask Cowork to** assemble a draft annual department report from your notes, last year's report, and a few supporting documents in a connected folder.
- **Ask Cowork to** turn a tangled budget spreadsheet into a clean summary — totals by category, a year-over-year comparison, the figures you actually present.
- **Ask Cowork to** draft a teaching-schedule grid for next term, or summarize a folder of meeting notes into a short list of action items with owners.

:::tip
The chair-admin tasks are the gentlest on-ramp: the inputs are yours, the right answer is checkable at a glance, and a wrong draft costs you nothing. Start here to build a feel for how Cowork plans and asks before you turn it loose on research.
:::
